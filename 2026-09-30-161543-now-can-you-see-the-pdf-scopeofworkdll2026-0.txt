● Phase 4 is done: the English Course now has a Reading section ("Read Up-Speed
  Up") that measures reading speed and comprehension. It works in the real
  student app and all server tests pass (487/487). Like phases 1–3 and the
  Content Exercises work, it's still uncommitted on feature/english-content.

  Where to find it: student app → menu (☰) → English Course → Reading.

  What's in it:
  - A short tutorial, "How to read faster": read in groups of words, find the
    main idea, read between the lines, and don't stop at unknown words. It has
    check questions along the way.
  - 12 original passages in 4 levels, getting longer as they get harder: A2
    (about 150 words) → B1 → B2 → C1 (300+ words).

  How a passage works:
  1. The student presses Start reading, and a timer runs while they read.
  2. They press I've finished reading. The passage then disappears.
  3. They answer 5 questions from memory: main idea, details, reading between
     the lines, and word meaning.
  4. The result shows:
     - words per minute, worked out by the server from the word count and
       reading time
     - comprehension %
     - effective speed (speed × comprehension), so fast reading only counts if
       the student understood it
     - every answer, with the right one
     - an option to read the text again

  There's also an optional pacer to help students speed up: a highlight moves
  through the text at a speed they choose (80–450 words/min), and they try to
  keep up.

  Progress: My progress now has a reading-speed chart with a legend, latest
  speed and best effective speed. Areas where students go wrong (e.g. "Reading
  for detail") link back to the tutorial.

  How I checked it: a test student in the browser:
  - Read the first passage using the pacer, then answered with one deliberate
    mistake: 80%, 319 words/min, effective speed 255. That matches about 160
    words read in 30 seconds.
  - Read a second passage without the pacer: 100%, 225 words/min.
  - Throughout, the passage stayed hidden during the questions, the progress
    chart showed both readings, and there were no page errors. All test data was
    removed.

  One thing to know: a student who clicks "finished" without really reading gets
  a very high speed. The comprehension score and effective speed expose this,
  but the system doesn't block it.

  follow-up on mistakes, writing worksheets) and phase 6 (teacher course builder
